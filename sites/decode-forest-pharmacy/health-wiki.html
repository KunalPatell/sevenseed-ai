Fundamental Framework

1. The Four Pillars of Human Longevity

Longevity and physical performance are governed by four foundational physiological systems. Attempting to optimize supplements before mastering energy balance is akin to tuning aerodynamics on a car without an engine.

1. Energy Balance

Thermodynamics dictating body composition. Caloric intake vs Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

2. Mitochondrial Density

Zone 2 aerobic base conditioning. Capillary density, VO2 max, and lactate clearance capacity.

3. Muscular Skeletons

Lean muscle mass preservation. Glycogen storage capacity, fall prevention, and metabolic reserve.

4. Sleep Restoration

Glymphatic brain clearance, growth hormone secretion, and autonomic nervous system regulation.

Thermodynamics & Macros

2. Nutrition Fundamentals & Protein Synthesis

The First Law of Thermodynamics applies universally: energy cannot be created or destroyed. Weight change is mathematically dictated by net caloric balance over rolling 14-day intervals.

Protein Requirements: For active individuals resistance training, optimal Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS) plateau occurs between 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of total body weight (0.73–1.0 g/lb). Intakes beyond 2.2 g/kg offer no additional muscle building benefits and are oxidized for energy.

Fat Minimums: Dietary fat is required for hormone synthesis (testosterone, estrogen), cellular membrane integrity, and fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) absorption. Never drop below 0.6 grams of fat per kg of body weight for extended periods.

Resistance Training Physiology

4. Hypertrophy & Strength Protocols

Muscle growth requires mechanical tension, muscle damage, and metabolic stress, with mechanical tension being the primary driver. Volume is quantified as weekly hard sets per muscle group taken within 0–3 Reps in Reserve (RIR).

Volume Landmarks

MEV (Minimum Effective Volume): 6–8 sets/week.
MRV (Max Recoverable Volume): 18–22 sets/week depending on sleep and nutrition.

Progressive Overload

Constantly challenge the neuromuscular system via increased load, extra repetitions, improved range of motion, or controlled eccentric tempo.

Aerobic Capacity

5. Cardiovascular Base: Zone 2 Training

Zone 2 training occurs at an intensity where blood lactate remains under 2.0 mmol/L, maximizing fat oxidation and stimulating mitochondrial biogenesis without inducing severe autonomic fatigue.

Prescription: 150–180 minutes per week divided into 3–4 sessions of steady-state cycling, running, rowing, or brisk incline walking at a conversational pace (roughly 65–75% of maximum heart rate).

Circadian Biology

6. Sleep Architecture & Neuro-Recovery

During slow-wave deep sleep (N3), the brain's glymphatic system contracts glial cells by 60%, allowing cerebrospinal fluid to wash away metabolic byproducts including beta-amyloid plaques.

Clinical Sleep Rules: Morning sunlight exposure within 30 minutes of waking sets the suprachiasmatic nucleus clock. Avoid caffeine 9 hours prior to bed. Keep ambient bedroom temperature at 18–20°C.
