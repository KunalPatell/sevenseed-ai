The Core Mental Models Index

Mathematics & Logic · Carl Jacobi

Inversion

Instead of asking how to achieve positive goals, invert the question: what would guarantee utter failure? Avoid that failure point systematically.

Example: Rather than "How do I build a viral product?", ask "What would make users instantly uninstall?"
Physics & Philosophy · Aristotle

First Principles Thinking

Boil a situation down to the most foundational truths that cannot be deduced any further, and reason up from there instead of by analogy.

Example: What does a battery really cost? Raw materials on the London Metal Exchange cost $80/kWh, not $600/kWh.
Economics & Strategy · Howard Marks

Second-Order Thinking

First-order thinking considers only immediate consequences ("This feels good now"). Second-order thinking asks: "And then what happens?"

Example: First-order: Slashing prices increases sales. Second-order: Slashing prices attracts low-LTV churners and destroys margins.
Philosophy & Science · William of Ockham

Occam's Razor

Among competing hypotheses, the one with the fewest assumptions is usually the correct one. Avoid over-engineering solutions.

Example: If an API is returning 500 errors, check database connection credentials before suspecting a kernel bug.
Engineering & Investing · Benjamin Graham

Margin of Safety

Always design structures and financial plans to withstand significantly more stress than predicted. Expect unexpected variance.

Example: If a bridge needs to hold 10-ton trucks, build it to support 30 tons.
Mathematics · Albert Einstein

Compounding

Small consistent gains of 1% every day yield 37x improvements over a year. The greatest results occur in the late tail of the curve.

Example: Writing 1 high-signal blog post or open-source commit per day builds unmatched domain authority over 3 years.
