Interactive Laws of UX Psychology

Interactive Physics Cards & Cognitive Heuristics
HeuristicPaul Fitts (1954)

Fitts's Law

T = a + b * log2(2D / W)

The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and width of the target.

Target Size: 48pxTarget Distance: 120px
Product Implication:Make actionable buttons large and position primary click zones within rapid reach.
CognitiveWilliam Edmund Hick (1952)

Hick's Law

RT = b * log2(n + 1)

The time it takes to make a decision increases logarithmically with the number and complexity of choices.

Interactive heuristic validator activeOptimal Range ✓
Product Implication:Break complex multi-choice menus into progressive disclosures and smart defaults.
CognitiveGeorge A. Miller (1956)

Miller's Law

Capacity = 7 ± 2 items

The average person can only keep 7 (plus or minus 2) items in their immediate working memory.

Interactive heuristic validator activeOptimal Range ✓
Product Implication:Chunk content into clusters of 5 to 7 elements to prevent working memory saturation.
PrincipleJakob Nielsen (2000)

Jakob's Law

Users spend most of their time on other sites. They expect your site to work the same way as all the others.

Target Size: 48pxTarget Distance: 120px
Product Implication:Use familiar navigation patterns, shopping carts, and UI paradigms rather than eccentric layouts.
CognitiveDaniel Kahneman (1993)

Peak-End Rule

People judge an experience largely based on how they felt at its peak and at its end, rather than total sum.

Interactive heuristic validator activeOptimal Range ✓
Product Implication:Deliver euphoric micro-animations on checkout completion and resolve errors with empathy.
PrincipleWalter J. Doherty (1982)

Doherty Threshold

Latency < 400ms

Productivity soars when a computer and its users interact at a pace (<400ms) that ensures neither has to wait.

Interactive heuristic validator activeOptimal Range ✓
Product Implication:Provide instantaneous optimistic UI responses and skeleton loaders under 400ms.
