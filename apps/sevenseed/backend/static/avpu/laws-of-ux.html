The 21 Core Laws of UX

Heuristic & Principle

Fitts's Law

The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and size of the target. Fast-action buttons should be large and pinned close to thumb reach.

Design Rule: Make primary CTAs large and position them along screen edges or thumb zones.
Cognitive Principle

Hick's Law

The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of options. Minimize choices in checkouts, nav menus, and pricing tiers.

Design Rule: Break complex workflows into multi-step wizards with 3 or fewer choices per step.
Memory & Perception

Miller's Law

The average person can hold only 7 (±2) chunks of information in working memory simultaneously.

Design Rule: Chunk phone numbers, credit cards, and dashboard metrics into clusters of 3-4 items.
Expectation Principle

Jakob's Law

Users spend most of their time on other sites. They expect your site to function identically to familiar mental models (e.g. cart on top right, logo on top left).

Design Rule: Innovate on core value, not on foundational navigation conventions.
Memory Biases

Peak-End Rule

People judge an experience based on how they felt at its emotional peak and at its conclusion, rather than the average sum of every moment.

Design Rule: Celebrate key milestones with confetti animations and ensure checkout ends on a high note.
Task Motivation

Zeigarnik Effect

People remember uncompleted or interrupted tasks significantly better than completed tasks. Incomplete progress bars create psychological tension to finish.

Design Rule: Show "75% profile completed" badges to trigger completion drive.
Visual Perception

Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Users perceive aesthetically pleasing designs as significantly more usable and are more forgiving of minor usability hiccups.

Design Rule: Premium micro-interactions and harmonious color palettes build immediate trust.
Visual Hierarchy

Von Restorff Effect

When multiple similar objects are present, the one that differs from the rest is most likely to be remembered.

Design Rule: Highlight "Recommended" pricing tiers with high-contrast colored borders.
Cognitive Principle

Goal-Gradient Effect

The tendency to approach a goal increases with proximity to the goal. Users accelerate action as the finish line comes in sight.

Design Rule: Start loyalty cards with 2 pre-stamped bonus stars to create perceived initial momentum.
