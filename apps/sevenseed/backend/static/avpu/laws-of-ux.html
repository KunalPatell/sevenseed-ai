The 21 Core Laws of UX

21 laws

Heuristic & Principle

Fitts's Law

The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and size of the target. Fast-action buttons should be large and pinned close to thumb reach.

Design Rule: Make primary CTAs large and position them along screen edges or thumb zones.
Deep Dive
Cognitive Principle

Hick's Law

The time it takes to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of options. Minimize choices in checkouts, nav menus, and pricing tiers.

Design Rule: Break complex workflows into multi-step wizards with 3 or fewer choices per step.
Deep Dive
Memory & Perception

Miller's Law

The average person can hold only 7 (±2) chunks of information in working memory simultaneously.

Design Rule: Chunk phone numbers, credit cards, and dashboard metrics into clusters of 3-4 items.
Deep Dive
Expectation Principle

Jakob's Law

Users spend most of their time on other sites. They expect your site to function identically to familiar mental models (e.g. cart on top right, logo on top left).

Design Rule: Innovate on core value, not on foundational navigation conventions.
Deep Dive
Memory Biases

Peak-End Rule

People judge an experience based on how they felt at its emotional peak and at its conclusion, rather than the average sum of every moment.

Design Rule: Celebrate key milestones with confetti animations and ensure checkout ends on a high note.
Deep Dive
Task Motivation

Zeigarnik Effect

People remember uncompleted or interrupted tasks significantly better than completed tasks. Incomplete progress bars create psychological tension to finish.

Design Rule: Show "75% profile completed" badges to trigger completion drive.
Deep Dive
Visual Perception

Aesthetic-Usability Effect

Users perceive aesthetically pleasing designs as significantly more usable and are more forgiving of minor usability hiccups.

Design Rule: Premium micro-interactions and harmonious color palettes build immediate trust.
Deep Dive
Visual Hierarchy

Von Restorff Effect

When multiple similar objects are present, the one that differs from the rest is most likely to be remembered.

Design Rule: Highlight "Recommended" pricing tiers with high-contrast colored borders.
Deep Dive
Cognitive Principle

Goal-Gradient Effect

The tendency to approach a goal increases with proximity to the goal. Users accelerate action as the finish line comes in sight.

Design Rule: Start loyalty cards with 2 pre-stamped bonus stars to create perceived initial momentum.
Deep Dive
Interaction Principle

Doherty Threshold

Productivity soars when a system responds to the user within 400ms, keeping both in flow with neither waiting on the other.

Design Rule: Show an optimistic UI update immediately, then reconcile with the server response.
Deep Dive
Decision Principle

Occam's Razor

Among competing designs that solve the problem equally well, the one with the fewest steps or elements should be chosen.

Design Rule: Before shipping a flow, try to delete a step — if nothing breaks, leave it deleted.
Deep Dive
Statistical Principle

Pareto Principle

Roughly 80% of outcomes come from 20% of causes — a small set of features usually drives most of the value users get.

Design Rule: Identify your top 20% of features by usage and give them the most prominent placement.
Deep Dive
Memory & Perception

Serial Position Effect

Users best remember the first and last items in a list or sequence, with middle items fading fastest from memory.

Design Rule: Put your most important nav items or pricing tiers at the start and end of a row.
Deep Dive
Systems Principle

Tesler's Law

Every system has an irreducible amount of complexity — the only question is whether the user or the developer absorbs it.

Design Rule: Push complexity into smart defaults and backend logic rather than onto the user's screen.
Deep Dive
Cognitive Principle

Cognitive Load

The amount of mental effort required to use an interface — every extra decision, label, or field adds to it.

Design Rule: Remove or hide any field, toggle, or choice that isn't needed for the current task.
Deep Dive
Memory & Perception

Chunking

Grouping individual pieces of information into larger, more meaningful units makes them easier to process and recall.

Design Rule: Break long forms and numbers into labeled groups of 3-4 fields instead of one long list.
Deep Dive
Gestalt Principle

Law of Proximity

Objects placed near each other are perceived as more related than objects placed further apart.

Design Rule: Tighten spacing between a label and its input; widen spacing between unrelated field groups.
Deep Dive
Gestalt Principle

Law of Similarity

Elements that share visual traits — color, shape, size — are perceived as more related than elements that don't.

Design Rule: Use one consistent button style for every primary action across the product.
Deep Dive
Cognitive Principle

Mental Model

Users carry expectations from other products into yours, based on what they already believe about how systems like it work.

Design Rule: Match established conventions (cart icon, hamburger menu) unless you have a strong reason to diverge.
Deep Dive
Attention Principle

Selective Attention

Users filter out most of what's on screen and focus only on what's relevant to their current goal.

Design Rule: Mute or remove secondary UI while a user is mid-task, like during checkout.
Deep Dive
Motivation Principle

Flow

The mental state of complete immersion in a task, reached when challenge and skill are closely matched.

Design Rule: Ramp difficulty or complexity gradually so users stay challenged without hitting frustration.
