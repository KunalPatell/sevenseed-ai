Arrest & Custody Rights
Right to Know Grounds of Arrest
Police must tell you why you are being arrested. Silent or unexplained arrest is illegal under BNSS 2023.
What You Are Entitled To
  • To be informed of the grounds of arrest immediately in a language you understand
  • Police must produce a memo of arrest signed by a witness or family member
  • Right to be informed of your right to bail if the offence is bailable
  • If arrested without warrant, you must be produced before Magistrate within 24 hours
What to Do
  • Calmly ask: "Please tell me the grounds of my arrest"
  • Do not resist physically — note officer name & badge number
  • Immediately inform a family member or lawyer
  • Ask for a copy of the arrest memo
BNSS § 47 BNSS § 48 Art. 22 Constitution

Failure to inform grounds of arrest makes the arrest illegal and entitles you to immediate release via writ of habeas corpus.

Right to Inform Family & Lawyer
You have a right to have one friend or relative informed of your arrest immediately — this is mandatory under BNSS 2023.
Entitlements
  • Immediate right to have one person of your choice informed of your arrest and location
  • Right to consult and be represented by a lawyer of your choice at your own expense
  • If unable to afford a lawyer — right to free legal aid under Legal Services Authorities Act
  • Right to speak privately with your lawyer before any police interrogation
BNSS § 48 Art. 22(1) Constitution Legal Services Act 1987

If police deny you the right to contact a lawyer or inform family, file a complaint with the Superintendent of Police or approach the State Human Rights Commission.

Right Against Self-Incrimination & Torture
You cannot be forced to confess. Confessions under coercion are inadmissible in court under BNS 2023.
Your Protections
  • Right to remain silent — you cannot be forced to answer questions that incriminate you
  • No confession made to a police officer is admissible in court (BNS § 23)
  • Any confession under torture, threat or inducement is invalid
  • Right to medical examination if you allege torture or assault in custody
  • Custodial torture is a criminal offence under BNS 2023
Art. 20(3) Constitution BNS § 23 BNS § 116

If tortured in custody, immediately inform your lawyer and approach the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) or file a PIL in the High Court.

Bail & Court Rights
Right to Bail in Bailable Offences
For bailable offences, bail is a right — not a privilege. Police cannot deny it.
Key Points
  • In bailable offences: bail must be granted by police or Magistrate as a matter of right
  • In non-bailable offences: Magistrate has discretion — apply for bail through a lawyer
  • Bail cannot be refused merely because you are poor or cannot arrange sureties
  • After 90 days (for serious offences) or 60 days (others) of custody with no chargesheet, you get default bail
BNSS § 478-494 BNS Schedule

If bail is refused in a bailable offence, file a revision petition before the Sessions Court immediately — no further delay is needed.

Right to Speedy Trial
Unreasonable delay in trial violates your fundamental rights. You can petition courts for speedy disposal.
Your Rights
  • Right to speedy trial is a fundamental right under Article 21
  • After 6 months of imprisonment without trial start, you may apply for bail as default
  • BNSS mandates time-bound trials for serious offences
  • You can petition the High Court for writ of mandamus to speed up trial
Art. 21 Constitution BNSS § 346
RTI & Information Rights
Right to Information (RTI) — How to File
Any citizen can seek information from government bodies within 30 days. RTI is your most powerful transparency tool.
Step-by-Step Process
1
Identify the Public Authority
Identify which ministry/department/office holds the information you need.
2
Draft Your Application
Write a clear, specific request on plain paper. No format is mandatory. Pay ₹10 fee (free for BPL).
3
Submit to PIO
Submit to Public Information Officer (PIO) by post, in person, or via RTI Online Portal (rtionline.gov.in).
4
Receive Response (30 days)
PIO must respond within 30 days (48 hours if life/liberty involved). If denied or delayed, file First Appeal.
RTI Act 2005 § 6 — Application § 7 — Disposal
Consumer Rights
6 Core Consumer Rights (Consumer Protection Act 2019)
Every Indian consumer has 6 statutory rights against unfair trade practices, defective products and deficient services.
Your 6 Rights
  • Right to Safety — Protection from hazardous goods & services
  • Right to Information — Quality, quantity, purity, price of goods
  • Right to Choose — Access to competitive products at fair prices
  • Right to be Heard — Your grievances must be addressed
  • Right to Redressal — Compensation for defective goods or services
  • Right to Consumer Education — Awareness of your rights
How to File a Consumer Complaint
  • File online at consumerhelpline.gov.in or National Consumer Helpline: 1800-11-4000
  • District Forum: Claims up to ₹1 Crore
  • State Commission: Claims ₹1 Cr – ₹10 Cr
  • National Commission (NCDRC): Claims above ₹10 Crore
Consumer Protection Act 2019 § 6 — Rights § 35 — Complaint
Digital Privacy Rights
Right to Privacy (Digital DPDP Act 2023)
India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 gives you the right to access, correct, and erase your personal data.
Your Data Rights
  • Right to know what data is collected about you and for what purpose
  • Right to correct inaccurate personal data held by companies
  • Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten") — request deletion of your data
  • Right to nominate someone to exercise your data rights after death
  • Right to grieve with Data Protection Board of India if rights violated
DPDP Act 2023 Art. 21 — Right to Privacy IT Act 2000 § 43A

If a company shares your personal data without consent, file a complaint with Data Protection Board India — penalties up to ₹250 Crore can be imposed on violators.

Workplace Rights
Right Against Workplace Harassment (POSH Act)
Every employee (especially women) has the right to a safe workplace free from sexual harassment under POSH Act 2013.
Rights Under POSH Act 2013
  • Right to file complaint with Internal Complaints Committee (ICC) within 3 months
  • Complaint must be resolved within 60 days (ICC inquiry + report)
  • Right to request transfer, leave, or other relief during inquiry
  • Right to appeal to Local Complaints Committee if no ICC exists in company
  • Whistleblower protection — employer cannot retaliate against complainant
POSH Act 2013 § 4 — ICC § 13 — Inquiry Report
Right to Minimum Wage & PF
Every worker is entitled to minimum wage, timely payment, and EPF deduction by employer — violations are criminal offences.
Worker Entitlements
  • Central minimum wage: ₹176–₹423/day depending on skill level (updated periodically)
  • Payment of wages must be made by 7th of next month (factories/establishments)
  • EPF deduction: 12% employee + 12% employer contribution mandatory if 20+ employees
  • Maternity benefit: 26 weeks paid leave for women in establishments with 10+ employees
  • Gratuity: 15 days salary per year of service after 5 years (Payment of Gratuity Act)
Minimum Wages Act 1948 EPF Act 1952 Maternity Benefit Act 1961
